## 1. T1 on upstream definitions

**The literature statement.** Let A be a complex abelian surface and K = ℚ(√−d) (d > 0) acting on A up to isogeny, with K acting on H^{1,0}(A) with each of its two embeddings once (A is of Weil type, n = 1). The Weil space W_K ⊂ H²(A; ℚ) is a two-dimensional space of Hodge classes, hence of algebraic classes by Lefschetz (1,1) ([Weil1977]; [vanGeemen2022] §4; exact locators unverified).

**The Weil space as an eigenspace.** Let f : A ⟶ A be an endomorphism whose pullback φ = f^* on H¹(A; ℚ) satisfies φ² = −d. Over ℂ, H¹ = V₊ ⊕ V₋ with V± = ker(φ ∓ i√d); complex conjugation exchanges V₊ and V₋ (φ is rational), so each has dimension 2. On H² = ∧²H¹ (true for abelian surfaces, [Mumford1970] §1, locator unverified) the pullback is ∧²φ, which acts by (i√d)² = −d on ∧²V₊ and on ∧²V₋, and by (i√d)(−i√d) = d on V₊ ∧ V₋. So the −d-eigenspace of f^* on H²(A; ℚ) is two-dimensional, with complexification ∧²V₊ ⊕ ∧²V₋. This is the complexification of W_K = ∧²_K H¹ in the cited sources (unverified locator). The ladder therefore defines

  weilSpace A f d := {w ∈ H²(A; ℚ) : f^* w = −d • w},

which needs pullback on H² and nothing about exterior algebras. On a surface where H² ≠ ∧²H¹ the definition is still meaningful; T1 only uses it on abelian surfaces and on surfaces where the span hypothesis of `weil-surface-reduction` holds.

**Why balance is a hypothesis.** If V₊ meets H^{1,0} in a line a and H^{0,1} in a line b, then ∧²V₊ = ℂ (a ∪ b) lies in H^{1,1}, and the same holds for V₋: W_K ⊂ Hdg^1. If instead V₊ = H^{1,0} (signature (2, 0)), then W_K ⊗ ℂ = H^{2,0} ⊕ H^{0,2}, so W_K ≠ 0 and W_K ∩ Hdg^1 = 0. The statement is false without balance. On upstream definitions, balance is stated as: F¹H¹_dR(A) meets both eigenspaces of the de Rham pullback (`IsWeilTypeSurface`, node G1).

**Equality first (decision 0004).** T1 is an inclusion in the literature (algebraicity of a subspace). On a surface it is equivalent to `weilSpace ≤ Hdg^1`, through the checked equality `lefschetzOneOneEquality_holds`. The plan proves the inclusion exactly; no scalar or sign enters, since W is defined by an eigenvalue and membership in F¹ ignores the factor order of `deRhamCup` (conventions row "de Rham cup order").

## 3. Route

**Split T1 into a reduction and its inputs.** The inventory's "by linear algebra" step is a conditional theorem on upstream definitions that can be checked soon: for any smooth projective integral surface A with an endomorphism of Weil type, if finrank H¹_dR(A) = 4 and H²_dR(A) is spanned by cup products of H¹ classes, then `weilSpace A f d ≤ Hdg^1`. Proof: the eigenvalues of the de Rham pullback on H¹ are ±i√d with two-dimensional eigenspaces; pick a ∈ F¹ ∩ V₊ and c ∈ F¹ ∩ V₋ and complete to bases {a, b}, {c, e}; the −d-eigenspace of f^* on H² lies in the span of the products of eigenvectors whose eigenvalues multiply to −d (`weil-eigen-algebra`); among these a ∪ a, b ∪ b, c ∪ c, e ∪ e vanish (`deRhamCup_self_of_odd`) and each other product has a factor in F¹ and one in F⁰ = ⊤, so lies in F¹ (`deRhamCup_mem_hodgeFiltration`). The hypotheses are statements on upstream definitions (rank, span, balance), as `docs/harness.md` requires of assumed inputs; there is no interface structure. This is node B, `weil-surface-reduction`, and it is the first rung of the T1 chain that a prover can reach after four small rungs.

**First instance: E × E with CM by ℤ[i].** The two remaining inputs (rank and span; a Weil-type endomorphism) need an actual surface. The cheapest is A = E × E with E : y²z = x³ − xz², which has CM by ℤ[i] through [i](x, y) = (−x, iy) ([Silverman2009], locator unverified), and f = [i] × [i]⁻¹, so d = 1 and K = ℚ(i). Reasons:

| Choice | Taken | Against |
|---|---|---|
| E as a closed subscheme of upstream ℙ² | projectivity comes from the closed immersion; charts are two affine hypersurfaces; the checked Segre glue and Mathlib's subscheme API both apply | Mathlib's `WeierstrassCurve` has no scheme over `Spec ℂ`; building one from it would also end in a closed subscheme of ℙ² |
| the specific cubic y² = x³ − x | the automorphism [i] is linear on ℙ², and the lattice is ℤ[i] (up to scaling), where g₃ = 0 is a reindexing | a general Weierstrass cubic needs the full j-invariant theory to pick its lattice |
| E × E | products, product charts and `smooth_prod` are checked; projectivity needs only a general Segre map | a general abelian surface needs A(ℂ) ≅ ℂ²/Λ for every `[GrpObj A]`, a Lie-group theorem absent at the pin |
| f = [i] × [i]⁻¹ | balanced: pr₁^*ω and pr₂^*ω are eigenvectors with eigenvalues ε and −ε, both in F¹; which of ±i is ε does not matter | f = [i] × [i] has signature (2, 0) and is the counterexample of §1 |
| CM eigenvalues in place of periods | F¹H¹(E) is a nonzero [i]-stable subspace of a 2-dimensional space, so it contains an eigenvector; no period integral, no Hodge symmetry | a period computation needs integration of forms against singular chains, absent at the pin |

The instance is a decomposable case: on E × E the Weil space is the K-part of H¹(E) ⊗ H¹(E), spanned by classes of graphs of 1 and [i] (unverified as a computation here). It pins the definitions on a surface with b₁ = 4; the exceptional Weil classes of the Markman program start in degree four (T3, T4). T1 for an instance and T1 in general share the reduction; only the topological input differs.

**General T1.** `weil-surface` needs the rank and span hypotheses for every abelian surface, which come from A(ℂ) ≅ ℂ²/Λ and the cohomology of a real 4-torus. The uniformization of complex abelian varieties is parked (`abelian-surface-uniformization`) until the instance is checked.

**Fallback route (divisors).** If the Hodge inputs of the instance die (kill criteria of D2 and D5), the instance can be proved without Hodge theory: show W ⊆ span{[Γ₁], [Γ_[i]], [pt × E], [E × pt]} by computing graph classes in the Künneth basis. This needs intersection numbers on E × E (cycle classes of curves paired against products of H¹ classes), which the ladder also lacks. It is recorded here and has no rung.

```lean
theorem pullbackCoh_comp {X Y Z : Over (Spec ↧ℂ)} (g : X ⟶ Y) (h : Y ⟶ Z) (n : ℕ)
    (α : H^n(Z; ℚ)) :
    Cup.pullbackCoh (g ≫ h) n α = Cup.pullbackCoh g n (Cup.pullbackCoh h n α)
theorem pullbackCoh_id (X : Over (Spec ↧ℂ)) (n : ℕ) (α : H^n(X; ℚ)) :
    Cup.pullbackCoh (𝟙 X) n α = α

    H^n(X; ℚ) →ₗ[ℚ] H^n(X'; ℚ) :=
  (AddMonoidHom.mk' (Cup.pullbackCoh g n) (Cup.pullbackCoh_add g n)).toRatLinearMap
```

The composition law is the work: `TopCat.Sheaf.pullback` along `pointMap (g ≫ h)` against the composite of inverse images, and the uniqueness of left adjoints behind `pullbackQX`. Size small to medium.

**A2 `weil-eigen-algebra`** (foundation, small; no dependency, Mathlib only). Elaborated:

```lean
theorem ker_sub_smul_le_span_of_eigen {K V W ι : Type*} [Field K] [AddCommGroup V]
    [Module K V] [AddCommGroup W] [Module K W] (B : V →ₗ[K] V →ₗ[K] W) (T : V →ₗ[K] V)
    (S : W →ₗ[K] W) (hS : ∀ x y, S (B x y) = B (T x) (T y)) (e : ι → V) (μ : ι → K)
    (he : ∀ i, T (e i) = μ i • e i)
    (hspan : Submodule.span K (Set.range fun p : ι × ι => B (e p.1) (e p.2)) = ⊤) (c : K) :
    LinearMap.ker (S - c • LinearMap.id) ≤
      Submodule.span K ((fun p : ι × ι => B (e p.1) (e p.2)) '' {p | μ p.1 * μ p.2 = c})
```

Proof: the B (e i) (e j) are eigenvectors of S with eigenvalue μ i μ j and span W, and eigenspaces for distinct eigenvalues are independent (Mathlib `Module.End.eigenspaces_iSupIndep`).

```lean
noncomputable def deRhamPullback (g : X ⟶ Y) (n : ℕ) : H_dR^n(Y) →ₗ[ℂ] H_dR^n(X) :=
  (deRhamBaseChangeEquiv X n).toLinearMap ∘ₗ (pullbackCohLinear g n).baseChange ℂ ∘ₗ
    (deRhamBaseChangeEquiv Y n).symm.toLinearMap
theorem deRhamPullback_fieldToDeRhamCohomology (n : ℕ) (α : H^n(Y; ℚ)) :
    deRhamPullback g n (fieldToDeRhamCohomology ℚ Y n α) =
      fieldToDeRhamCohomology ℚ X n (Cup.pullbackCoh g n α)
theorem deRhamPullback_cup {a b n : ℕ} (h : a + b = n) (x : H_dR^a(Y)) (y : H_dR^b(Y)) :
    deRhamPullback g n (deRhamCup x y n (by lia)) =
      deRhamCup (deRhamPullback g a x) (deRhamPullback g b y) n (by lia)
theorem deRhamPullback_conj (n : ℕ) (x : H_dR^n(Y)) :
```

**G1 `weil-surface-statement`** (statement, small; depends on `weil-space`, `de-rham-pullback`). Elaborated, including the instance search for `[GrpObj A]`:

```lean
def IsWeilTypeSurface (A : Over (Spec ↧ℂ)) [IsIntegral A.left] [Smooth A.hom]
    [IsProjective A.hom] (f : A ⟶ A) (d : ℕ) : Prop :=
  0 < d ∧ (∀ x : H^1(A; ℚ), Cup.pullbackCoh f 1 (Cup.pullbackCoh f 1 x) = -(d : ℚ) • x) ∧
    F^1 H_dR^1(A) ⊓
      LinearMap.ker (deRhamPullback f 1 - (Complex.I * (Real.sqrt d : ℂ)) • LinearMap.id) ≠ ⊥ ∧
    F^1 H_dR^1(A) ⊓
      LinearMap.ker (deRhamPullback f 1 + (Complex.I * (Real.sqrt d : ℂ)) • LinearMap.id) ≠ ⊥

def WeilClassesAlgebraicSurface : Prop :=
  ∀ (A : Over (Spec ↧ℂ)) [IsIntegral A.left] [Smooth A.hom] [IsProjective A.hom] [GrpObj A],
    dim A.left = 2 → ∀ (f : A ⟶ A) (d : ℕ), IsWeilTypeSurface A f d →
      weilSpace A f d ≤ algebraicCycleClassSpan A 1
```

On an abelian surface with F¹H¹ two-dimensional and f-stable, the two balance conditions say that K acts on H^{1,0} with signature (1, 1). A red-team pass precedes posting: vacuity (no abelian surface at the pin), the (2, 0) counterexample of §1, the sign −d, and a new conventions row "Weil space" (pullback direction, sign of the eigenvalue, the root i√d).
