## Findings

1. **Correctness for Re τ ≠ 0 (attack 1).** The statement quantifies over all τ : ℂ and the proof makes no case split on τ.re. Its route is the one the brief describes: on the top side x + (a.im + Im τ) I = (x − Re τ) + a.im I + τ (`linear_combination re_add_im τ`), so period τ turns the top integrand into the bottom one shifted by Re τ; `integral_comp_sub_right` moves the window to [a.re − Re τ, a.re − Re τ + 1], and `Function.Periodic.intervalIntegral_add_eq` (period 1, no integrability) moves it back to [a.re, a.re + 1]. The vertical sides agree pointwise by period 1. An instance where Re τ matters, Probe P4: h(x + yi) = exp(2πi(x − y/2)) has periods 1 and 1/2 + I and lacks period I, so the theorem at τ = 1/2 + I covers a function the purely imaginary case (τ = I) would miss.

2. **Statement against the literature (item 1).** The literature fact is: the boundary integral of an elliptic function over the boundary of a fundamental parallelogram {a + t₁ω₁ + t₂ω₂ : 0 ≤ tᵢ ≤ 1} vanishes, because opposite sides are lattice translates traversed in opposite directions [`Silverman2009`, ch. VI, the proof that the residues in a fundamental parallelogram sum to 0; proposition number unverified]. Field by field against the Lean: the function is any h : ℂ → E with h(p + 1) = h p and h(p + τ) = h p for all p (the literature needs only the two periods, and no holomorphy, for this step); the domain is a rectangle of width 1 and height Im τ in place of the parallelogram spanned by 1 and τ. With ω₁ = 1 real, the horizontal sides of the rectangle are translates by τ up to a shift by Re τ along the period-1 direction, which `intervalIntegral_add_eq` absorbs, so the rectangle form is the parallelogram form sheared along the real axis. The value is exactly 0. For non-real ω₁ the rectangle form fails in general; sub-rung 6 (`period-pair-normalize`) brings every lattice to ω₁ = 1, so the consumer works with ω₁ = 1 only.

3. **Hypotheses satisfiable, and each used (item 2, item 3).** h = 0 satisfies both trivially; P4 is a nonconstant instance with Re τ ≠ 0; Mathlib's ℘ gives the consumer's instance (P3). `boundary_im_ne_zero` shows h₁ alone is insufficient: h = Im has period 1, the expression at a = 0, τ = I is −1. Probe P1 confirms that test is literally the theorem's left side at h = Im, a = 0, τ = I (term-mode `example` with `let` bindings, accepted). Probe P2 adds the symmetric test: h = Re has period I and lacks period 1, and the expression at a = 0, τ = I is I, so h₁ is used too (optional change O1). The conclusion is an equation between a sum of four interval integrals and 0; no definition unfolds to `True`, no coercion or empty type enters, and the two tests show it fails when either hypothesis is dropped. I did not find a proof that avoids both hypotheses, and P1, P2 show none exists.

4. **Fundamental domain and the counting step (attack 2).** Sub-rung 5 (`lattice-fundamental-rectangle`) uses the half-open rectangle `Ico a.re (a.re + 1) ×ℂ Ico a.im (a.im + L.ω₂.im)` with height `L.ω₂.im`, the height of this rung at τ = ω₂. It is a fundamental domain of Λ = ℤ + ℤω₂ when ω₁ = 1: for z, the imaginary part of z + m + nω₂ is z.im + n · Im ω₂, so n is the unique `toIcoDiv` in the imaginary direction (period Im ω₂ > 0); then m is the unique `toIcoDiv` of the real part with period 1. Uniqueness of (m, n) gives uniqueness of the shift because 1 and ω₂ are ℝ-independent. No shear is needed in the statement; the shear only explains why the area is the covolume. The counting step (sub-rung 7) needs, beyond this rung and sub-rungs 3 and 5:
   - (a) `logDeriv (fun p ↦ ℘ p − c)` periodic under 1 and ω₂ at every p, junk values included. Probe P3 proves it from `PeriodPair.weierstrassP_add_coe` and `deriv_comp_add_const` (the function is periodic everywhere, so its derivative is), then closes `rectIntegral (logDeriv fun p ↦ L.weierstrassP p - c) a (a + 1 + L.ω₂.im * I) = 0` from `L.ω₁_mem_lattice`, `L.ω₂_mem_lattice` and h₁ : L.ω₁ = 1.
   - (b) Corner arithmetic: sub-rung 3 states its hypotheses and its finsum on `[[z.re, w.re]] ×ℂ [[z.im, w.im]]` and its open interior with w = a + 1 + ω₂.im * I, while sub-rung 5 writes `a.re + 1`, `a.im + L.ω₂.im`. The bridge is `(a + 1 + τ.im * I).re = a.re + 1` and `.im = a.im + τ.im`, both `by simp` (the candidate's `hre`, `him`), plus `uIcc_of_le` with 0 < 1 and 0 < Im ω₂.
   - (c) The single lattice point: the half-open rectangle holds exactly one point of Λ (uniqueness of the shift of 0), and the boundary misses Λ (S ⊇ Λ), so it lies in the open rectangle; its divisor for ℘ − c is −2 (Mathlib's order of ℘ at lattice points, unchanged by subtracting a constant from a pole).
   - (d) Incongruence and covering of F: c-points in the open rectangle are pairwise incongruent by uniqueness, and every c-class meets it by existence plus the boundary missing S + Λ.
   None of these is a gap in this rung; (a) and (b) go on the consumer's note (required change 4).
