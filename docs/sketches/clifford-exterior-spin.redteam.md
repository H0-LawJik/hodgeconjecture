## 1. Is this the statement the literature makes?

[Markman2025], §"The Clifford algebra and the spin group" (arXiv source, quoted): the lattice V = H¹(X, ℤ) ⊕ H¹(X, ℤ)^* carries
"((w_1,θ_1),(w_2,θ_2))_V := θ_1(w_2) + θ_2(w_1)";
"C(V) [is] the quotient of the tensor algebra ... by the relation v_1·v_2 + v_2·v_1 = (v_1,v_2)_V";
"Given w ∈ H¹(X,ℤ), define ... L_w(•) = w ∧ (•). Given θ ∈ H¹(X,ℤ)^* we get the endomorphism D_θ ... given by contraction with θ ... m_{(w,θ)} := L_w + D_θ ... satisfying ... m_{v_1}∘m_{v_2} + m_{v_2}∘m_{v_1} = (v_1,v_2)_V · id_S ... Hence, m extends an algebra homomorphism m : C(V) → End(S), which is in fact an isomorphism, by [GLO, Prop. 3.2.1(e)]."
Here S = H*(X, ℤ) = ∧*H¹(X, ℤ), S⁺ = H^ev, S⁻ = H^odd.

[GolyshevLuntsOrlov2001], §3.1 (quoted): Λ = Γ ⊕ Γ^* with "Q((a_1,b_1),(a_2,b_2)) = b_1(a_2) + b_2(a_1)" and "Cl(Λ,Q) := T(Λ)/⟨x⊗x = ½ Q(x,x)⟩", so x² = b(a) for x = (a, b). §3.2.1(d) identifies the spinor module (the left ideal I generated by l₁⋯l_{2n}) with ∧Γ^* = ∧H¹; under that identification Γ^* acts by left multiplication and Γ by contraction. §3.5.1: I^ev and I^odd are the two half-spin representations.

## 2. Can the hypotheses be satisfied?

There are no hypotheses beyond `[CommRing R] [AddCommGroup M] [Module R M]`; ℚ over ℚ and ℤ over ℤ satisfy them. The parity theorem's hypotheses (`x ∈ evenOdd _ i`, `y ∈ exteriorParity _ _ j`) are met non-trivially. Scratch file, all compiled with no `sorry` (R = M = ℚ):

- `ExteriorAlgebra.ι ℚ (1 : ℚ) ∉ exteriorParity ℚ ℚ 0`, from `ι_mem_evenOdd_one`, `CliffordAlgebra.evenOdd_isCompl` and `ExteriorAlgebra.ι_eq_zero_iff`. So the parity parts are proper.
- `spinAction ℚ ℚ (ι _ (0, 1)) 1 ≠ 0 ∧ spinAction ℚ ℚ (ι _ (0, 1)) 1 ∈ exteriorParity ℚ ℚ 1`, from `spinAction_ι_one` and `spinAction_mem_exteriorParity` applied to `1 ∈ exteriorParity ℚ ℚ 0` (`SetLike.GradedOne.one_mem`).
- For every R, M: an element of `CliffordAlgebra.even (dualProd R M)` preserves `exteriorParity R M j`, from `spinAction_mem_exteriorParity` and `even_toSubmodule`. This is the half-spin form of the statement.

## 3. Is the conclusion trivially true?

Attempts, all of which failed to trivialize anything:

- `spinAction` built trivially? It is `CliffordAlgebra.lift` of an explicit operator, and any algebra hom with `φ (ι v) = spinGen R M v` equals it (scratch: `CliffordAlgebra.hom_ext`, four lines). `spinGen` is nonzero: `spinAction_ι_one` sends 1 to ι x, and `ExteriorAlgebra.ι_eq_zero_iff` makes that nonzero for x ≠ 0.

- `exteriorParity_eq_evenOdd` is an unfolding (`rw [exteriorParity, evenOdd, iSup_subtype']`). It is a bridge between two spellings, labelled as such, and carries no claim of its own.

## 7. Equality first

`spinGen_mul_self` is an identity, proved exactly: `spinGen R M v * spinGen R M v = algebraMap R _ (QuadraticForm.dualProd R M v)`, no scalar. Scratch: the polarized form `spinGen v * spinGen w + spinGen w * spinGen v = algebraMap R _ (v.1 w.2 + w.1 v.2)` follows in five lines from `ι_mul_ι_add_swap`; it is Markman's relation verbatim and worth stating (change 3). The parity theorem is a membership statement; the equality it steps toward is Chevalley's `Cl(dualProd R M) ≅ End(∧M)` for M finite free, with the half-spin decomposition `Cl⁰ ≅ End(∧^ev) × End(∧^odd)` after it. The first exists as the open rung `clifford-exterior-chevalley`; the second has no rung.

## 9. Verdict: pass with changes

Findings and changes, in order of weight:

1. **Title overclaims.** "acts on the exterior algebra ∧*H as its spin module" is supported only in the weak sense of "is a Cl-module". In [GLO] §3 and in [Markman2025] the identification of ∧*H with the spin module, and Markman's "S is the Spin representation", rest on the isomorphism C(V) ≅ End(S) [GLO 3.2.1(e)], which is the open rung. Retitle to: "The Clifford algebra of the split form on H^∨ ⊕ H acts on ∧*H by contraction plus wedge, with v² = q(v) (sign +, no factor 2), compatible with parity", and put "∧*H is the spin module: Cl(V) ≅ End(∧*H)" in the title of `clifford-exterior-chevalley`. The module docstring's first paragraph ("this is the action ... used in Markman's construction") is accurate as written.
2. **Add the non-vacuity lemma for parity** (section 7b): `ExteriorAlgebra.ι R m ∉ exteriorParity R M 0` for m ≠ 0 over a nontrivial R, and name it in the ledger's non-vacuity sentence.
3. **Add the polarized relation** `spinGen v * spinGen w + spinGen w * spinGen v = algebraMap R _ (v.1 w.2 + w.1 v.2)`, Markman's form of the Clifford relation, and the uniqueness lemma (any algebra hom agreeing with `spinGen` on generators is `spinAction`). Both compiled in scratch.
4. **`depends_on: lefschetz-1-1-equality`** is a sequencing dependency (decision 0003), not a mathematical one. Say so in the note, or the website's dependency graph will read it as an input.
5. **Generality is right.** Downstream needs ℤ: Markman's S = H*(X, ℤ), integral C(V) and Spin(V), and GLO work over ℤ. A field would not serve them. The Chevalley rung's hypothesis (M finite free) holds for H¹ of a complex torus with ℤ or ℚ coefficients.
6. **Chevalley route is sound.** Mathlib has the Orzech property for every commutative ring (`RingTheory/FiniteType.lean`, around line 600) and `OrzechProperty.bijective_of_surjective_of_finrank_le` (`LinearAlgebra/Dimension/Free.lean:374`), so surjectivity plus a spanning set of 2^{2n} elements gives bijectivity. This is GLO's proof of 3.2.1(e) (rank count plus surjectivity via matrix units). An alternative for the spanning set: induct on the rank with `QuadraticForm.dualProdProdIsometry` and `CliffordAlgebra.prodEquiv` (graded tensor product, `CliffordAlgebra/Prod.lean`), reducing to rank one, where Cl(dualProd R R) is spanned by 1, e, f, ef. Record the alternative in the rung's note.
7. **Upstreaming fitness.** `contractLeft_mem_range_ι_pow` and `contractLeft_mem_evenOdd` belong in Mathlib's `CliffordAlgebra/Contraction.lean`; for Mathlib, R and M should be implicit (they are explicit through `variable (R M)`, which reads badly when Q is given). `exteriorParity` duplicates `evenOdd 0` and would be dropped upstream. `spinAction` is a reasonable Mathlib addition under a name such as `CliffordAlgebra.dualProdExteriorAction`, with "spin" left for the Chevalley isomorphism. None of this blocks the rung.

No finding touches the truth of a checked statement. Attempts that failed: trivializing parity through `⊤`, building a second action with the opposite sign, and finding a Q = 0 restriction where the literature is general.
